Uttarakhand: A Brief History

Uttarakhand is often called the "Land of the Gods" because of its ancient Hindu shrines, sacred rivers, and Himalayan mythology. The region has been a spiritual destination for pilgrims since Vedic times, with the famous Char Dham shrines of Kedarnath, Badrinath, Gangotri, and Yamunotri drawing visitors for centuries.

During the 19th century, the British developed many hill stations such as Mussoorie and Nainital as summer retreats, adding colonial architecture and scenic roadways that still charm travelers today. The forests and wildlife of Uttarakhand were later protected through national parks like Jim Corbett, India's oldest tiger reserve.

In 2000, Uttarakhand became India's 27th state, formed from the hilly northern districts of Uttar Pradesh to better preserve its mountain culture and environment. Today it balances pilgrimage, adventure, and eco-tourism, while still honoring the traditions and natural beauty that made it famous.

Delhi to Uttarakhand Road Map

Choose the best route and daily highlights for a comfortable trip from Delhi into the hills of Uttarakhand.

2-Day Itinerary

  • Day 1: Drive from Delhi to Rishikesh (approx. 6-7 hrs).
  • Evening: Ganga aarti at Triveni Ghat and a riverside stay.
  • Day 2: Explore Laxman Jhula, Beatles Ashram, and return to Delhi.

3-Day Itinerary

  • Day 1: Delhi to Haridwar, visit Har Ki Pauri and local bazaars.
  • Day 2: Head to Rishikesh for rafting, yoga, and riverside cafes.
  • Day 3: Morning Ganga outing and drive back to Delhi.

4-Day Itinerary

  • Day 1: Delhi to Rishikesh, relax by the Ganges.
  • Day 2: Rishikesh sightseeing and evening yoga session.
  • Day 3: Drive to Mussoorie, walk on Mall Road and visit Gun Hill.
  • Day 4: Morning view at Lal Tibba and return to Delhi.

5-Day Itinerary

  • Day 1: Delhi to Rishikesh, evening Ganga aarti.
  • Day 2: Adventure in Rishikesh, rafting or trekking.
  • Day 3: Travel to Nainital, enjoy Lake Naini and local markets.
  • Day 4: Visit Snow View Point and Sattal or Bhimtal.
  • Day 5: Drive back to Delhi with roadside stops.

6-Day Itinerary

  • Day 1: Delhi to Rishikesh for evening aarti.
  • Day 2: Trek or rafting in Rishikesh.
  • Day 3: Drive to Mussoorie, explore the mall and waterfalls.
  • Day 4: Head to Nainital, boating on Naini Lake.
  • Day 5: Visit Jim Corbett or a nearby wildlife gateway.
  • Day 6: Return to Delhi via Meerut or Ghaziabad.

Hardoi to Uttarakhand Train Road Map

This route is designed for travelers starting from Hardoi, Uttar Pradesh, and taking trains to reach Uttarakhand safely and comfortably.

Train Route Overview

  • Step 1: Hardoi to Lucknow Charbagh. Many passenger and express trains connect these stations in about 2–3 hours.
  • Step 2: Lucknow to Moradabad Junction. Moradabad is the main rail gateway toward Uttarakhand and takes around 5–6 hours.
  • Step 3: Moradabad to Haridwar. This short train ride is typically 1.5–2 hours and brings you to the pilgrimage gateway.
  • Step 4: From Haridwar, take a taxi or bus to Rishikesh (20–30 minutes) or continue by train toward Dehradun if needed.

Suggested 3-Day Uttarakhand Plan

  • Day 1: Hardoi → Lucknow → Moradabad → Haridwar. Arrive in the evening and attend Ganga Aarti.
  • Day 2: Explore Haridwar in the morning, then travel to Rishikesh for sightseeing at Laxman Jhula, Beatles Ashram, and riverside cafes.
  • Day 3: Option to visit Dehradun or Mussoorie from Rishikesh, then return to Hardoi by train via Moradabad and Lucknow.

Alternative Uttarakhand Extensions

  • If you'd like Nainital: take Hardoi → Lucknow → Bareilly → Kathgodam, then road transfer to Nainital.
  • If you want a shorter trip: stay 1 night in Haridwar and 1 night in Rishikesh before returning.
  • For wildlife and nature: continue from Rishikesh to Jim Corbett or Kausani for a more scenic mountain route.
